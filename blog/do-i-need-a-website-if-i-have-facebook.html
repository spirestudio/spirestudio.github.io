Do I need a website if I'm already on Facebook? An honest answer.

Plenty of salons, barbers and cafes around Lichfield do well with just a Facebook page or an Instagram account. So it's a fair question: why bother with a website at all? The honest answer is that social media is great for keeping existing customers, but it's weak at winning new ones.

Where social media works well

  • Showing off your latest work, like fresh cuts, new nails or today's specials
  • Keeping regulars up to date with offers and closures
  • Getting word of mouth going when customers share and tag you

If most of your bookings come from people who already know you, social media does a lot of the job.

Where it lets you down

People search Google, not Facebook

When someone new to the area wants a barber or a plumber, they type "barbers near me" into Google. Facebook pages rarely show up near the top of those searches. A proper website, with your services, town and opening hours written clearly, gives Google something to rank.

Not everyone has an account

Instagram often asks visitors to log in after a few seconds of browsing. Lots of people, especially older customers, simply leave. A website opens for everyone, instantly.

You don't own your page

Your Facebook page lives on Facebook's terms. Accounts get hacked, locked or buried by algorithm changes overnight. Your website and web address belong to you.

It's hard to find the basics

Prices, opening hours and your address end up scattered across old posts. A website puts the things customers actually need on one clean page, with a button to call or message you.

The best setup: both, working together

You don't have to choose. Keep posting on social media, and link it to a simple website that does three jobs:

  1. Shows up when new customers search Google for what you do in your area
  2. Answers the basic questions: what you offer, prices, hours and where you are
  3. Makes it easy to get in touch with one tap, by phone or WhatsApp

Then put your website link in your Instagram and Facebook bios, so everything points to one place.
