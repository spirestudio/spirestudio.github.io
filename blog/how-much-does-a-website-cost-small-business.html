How much does a website cost for a small business? Real prices for 2026.

If you run a cafe, salon, barbers or trade business, you've probably wondered what a website actually costs. Quotes seem to range from "free" to thousands of pounds, and it's hard to tell what you're paying for. Here's a plain breakdown of your options in 2026, so you can make the call yourself.

The short answer

For a small local business, a good website usually costs somewhere between £100 and £1,500 to set up, plus a small amount each year to keep it online. Where you land depends on who builds it and how much you want them to look after afterwards.

OptionTypical costGood for
Do it yourself (Wix, Squarespace)£10 to £30 a monthPeople with spare evenings who enjoy fiddling
Local freelancer or small studio£100 to £1,500 one-offMost cafes, salons, barbers and trades
Agency£2,000 and upLarger firms needing online shops or booking systems

Doing it yourself

Website builders like Wix and Squarespace look cheap at first. The catch is time. Most owners we speak to started one, got halfway, and never finished because the business came first. You also keep paying the monthly fee for as long as the site is up, so over three years a £20-a-month plan costs £720.

If you enjoy design and have the time, it's a fair option. If not, a half-finished site can put customers off more than no site at all.

Using a local freelancer or studio

This is where most small businesses end up, and for good reason. You get a site designed around your business, built by someone who will pick up the phone. Prices vary a lot, so ask exactly what's included:

  • Is it designed for your business, or a template with your logo swapped in?
  • Does it work properly on phones? Most of your customers will look on one.
  • Who owns the website and the web address once it's paid for?
  • What does it cost each year to keep it online?
  • Can you get small changes made, like new prices or opening hours?

Going with an agency

Agencies are worth it when you need something complex, like an online shop with hundreds of products or a custom booking system. For a local business that mainly needs customers to find you, call you and see your work, you'd be paying for capacity you won't use.

The costs people forget

  • Your web address (domain). A .co.uk address is usually £5 to £15 a year. Make sure it's registered in your name.
  • Hosting. This keeps the site online. Some builders include it, others charge monthly.
  • Updates. Prices change, menus change, staff change. Check whether changes are free, included in a plan, or charged per job.
  • A professional email. Something like hello@yourbusiness.co.uk looks far more trustworthy than a Hotmail address.
