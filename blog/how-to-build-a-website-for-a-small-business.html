How to build a website for your small business. A step-by-step UK guide.

Building a website for your business is very doable, even if you've never done it before. This guide walks through every step in order, from picking your web address to getting found on Google. Follow it and you'll end up with a site that brings in customers, not just one that exists.

Step 1: Decide what the website is for

Before touching any tools, write down the one thing you want visitors to do. For most local businesses it's one of these:

  • Call or WhatsApp you (trades, plumbers, electricians)
  • Book an appointment (salons, barbers, groomers)
  • Visit in person (cafes, shops, takeaways)

Every page you build should push people towards that one action. It keeps the site simple and stops you adding things nobody needs.

Step 2: Get your web address (domain)

Your domain is your address online, like yourbusiness.co.uk. A few tips:

  • Use .co.uk if your customers are in the UK. It tells people and Google you're local.
  • Keep it short and easy to say out loud. Avoid hyphens and numbers if you can.
  • Register it in your own or your business's name, so it always belongs to you.

Expect to pay around £5 to £15 a year from registrars like Porkbun, Namecheap or 123 Reg.

Step 3: Choose how you'll build it

You have three realistic options:

  1. A website builder like Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy. Drag and drop, no code, monthly fee. Good if you have time to learn it.
  2. WordPress. Very flexible and powerful, but there's more to set up and keep updated. Better for people who like tinkering.
  3. Hire a web designer. Someone builds it for you. Costs more up front, but saves you the time and usually looks more professional.

Not sure which? We compare them in Wix vs Squarespace vs a web designer.

Step 4: Plan your pages

A small business doesn't need a huge site. One well-built page can do the job. If you want more, these are the pages that matter:

  • Home: what you do, where, and a big button to contact you
  • Services and prices: what you offer and roughly what it costs
  • About: who you are, how long you've been going, why people choose you
  • Reviews: real quotes from real customers
  • Contact: phone, WhatsApp, address, opening hours and a map

Step 5: Take good photos

Photos make or break a small business website. Use your own, not stock images. You don't need a professional; a recent phone in good daylight is plenty. Take shots of your premises from outside, your team at work, and your best work up close. Clear away clutter first.

Step 6: Write the words

Write like you talk to customers. Short sentences, no jargon. Make sure these appear clearly:

  • What you do and the town you're in, near the top (for example, "Family-run barbers in Lichfield")
  • Your phone number and opening hours
  • Why someone should pick you over the place down the road

Mentioning your town and area naturally in your text helps you appear in local searches.

Step 7: Make sure it works on phones

Most of your visitors will be on a phone. Check every page on yours: can you read it without zooming, are the buttons easy to tap, does anything slide off the edge? Add a tap-to-call or WhatsApp button that's always visible.

Step 8: Get it on Google

  1. Set up your free Google Business Profile, so you appear on Google Maps.
  2. Add your website to Google Search Console and submit your sitemap, so Google finds every page.
  3. Ask happy customers for Google reviews.

Step 9: Launch checklist

  • Every button and link works, especially phone and WhatsApp
  • Opening hours and prices are correct
  • It loads quickly on mobile data
  • Your website link is in your Instagram, Facebook and Google profile
  • You've added a privacy policy if you collect any customer details
