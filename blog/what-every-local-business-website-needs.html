7 things every local business website needs. And most are missing.

A website doesn't need to be big to work. For a local business it has one job: turn someone looking at their phone into a customer walking through your door. These are the seven things that make that happen. If your site is missing a few, they're usually quick fixes.

1. It works properly on a phone

Most people will find you on their phone, often while they're out and about. Text should be readable without zooming, buttons big enough to tap, and nothing should slide off the side of the screen. Open your own site on your phone right now. If it's fiddly, customers are leaving.

2. A tap-to-call or WhatsApp button

Don't make people copy your number. A big, obvious button to call or message you, ideally one that stays at the bottom of the screen, is the single biggest thing for getting enquiries.

3. Opening hours, front and centre

"Are they open now?" is one of the most common reasons people look you up. Put your hours where they can't be missed. Even better, show whether you're open right now.

4. Your address and a map

Write your full address, including the town and postcode, and link to directions. It helps customers find you and helps Google understand where you are, which matters for local searches.

5. Real photos of your business

Stock photos make every business look the same. Photos of your actual shop, your team and your work build trust straight away. A few good phone photos in daylight beat any stock image.

6. Your reviews

People trust other customers more than they trust you. Show off your Google rating and a handful of real reviews. If you've got 4.8 stars from 120 people, say so near the top.

7. Clear prices or a menu

You don't need to list everything, but a price list or "from" prices stops people guessing and saves you answering the same questions all day. Customers who know roughly what it'll cost are much more likely to get in touch.

Bonus: your own web address

yourbusiness.co.uk looks more professional than a long free address, and it's yours to keep. It usually costs under £15 a year.
